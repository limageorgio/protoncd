The Challenge

Complex Projects Need
One Independent Technical Voice

In Brazil, the distance between a signed contract and a safely accepted asset is filled with technical decisions, regulatory interfaces and undocumented agreements. Independent owner's representation and third-party management exist to close that gap — with documented decisions and verifiable evidence. Without them, those gaps become the owner's risk.

Where projects lose control

  • Layered supply chains — global equipment suppliers, national EPC contractors, local installers and specialized service providers, each with a different scope boundary and language.
  • Regulatory translation — ABNT NBR standards, NRs, municipal building codes, fire authority requirements and CREA/ART obligations rarely map one-to-one to international practice.
  • Documentation drift — site instructions, scope changes and verbal agreements that never reach the contract, the drawing set or the acceptance record.
  • Subjective acceptance — handover decided by schedule pressure instead of objective criteria, leaving the owner with unresolved items and an unclear warranty position.

Risk & Escalation

A Five-Level
Escalation Ladder

Disagreements are normal. Undocumented disagreements are dangerous. Every finding follows a defined path from observation to executive decision.

Level 1 · Observation

The condition is identified during a site visit or document review and recorded in the field log with photos, location and applicable requirement.

Level 2 · Formal notification

The contractor and the owner receive a written notification describing the condition, the violated requirement and the recommended correction.

Level 3 · NCR with deadline

The finding becomes a tracked non-conformance with an owner, a corrective action, a due date and a verification method. The clock starts.

Level 4 · Executive escalation

If the deadline is missed or the response is insufficient, the issue is escalated to contractor and owner leadership with the complete evidence trail.

Level 5 · Documented stop of our own team

For imminent risk, Proton withdraws its own team from the affected area and issues a circumstantial record. We do not command contractor crews — we protect our people and document the refusal or omission.

Safety is a communication duty, not a command authority

We have the technical duty to identify, register, notify and escalate unsafe conditions. We do not replace the contractor's legal responsibility for its workers, its equipment or its means and methods. This distinction is stated in the governance charter and reinforced at every site induction.
