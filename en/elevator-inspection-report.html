Applicable Standards & Codes

ASME A17.1-2022
Safety Code for Elevators and Escalators. Reference standard used in parallel with Brazilian ABNT norms for multinational client reporting.

ASME A17.2-2020
Guide for Inspection of Elevators, Escalators, and Moving Walks. Defines inspection procedures and acceptance criteria used in our two-phase protocol.

ISO 18738:2012
Measurement of Ride Quality in Lifts / Elevators. Horizontal vibration A95 ≤ 15 milli-g, cab noise ≤ 55 dB(A). All parameters measured with RBC-calibrated instruments.

ABNT NBR 16483
Brazilian standard for safety rules for the construction and installation of electric elevators (equivalent to EN 81-20). Mandatory for ART issuance.

ABNT NBR 15597
Brazilian standard for safety rules for existing elevators (equivalent to EN 81-80). Applied in our normative compliance audit of in-service units.

ASME A17.3-2020
Safety Code for Existing Elevators and Escalators. Used as a retrofit and modernization compliance benchmark for aged installations.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Brazil follow ASME A17.1 for elevator safety?
Brazil has its own national standard, ABNT NBR 16483 (new installations) and ABNT NBR 15597 (existing elevators), which are structurally aligned with ASME A17.1 and EN 81-20/50 in scope and intent. For multinational clients and cross-border legal proceedings, Proton Engineering applies ASME A17.1 and ASME A17.2 as parallel benchmarks, ensuring bilingual documentation accepted by both Brazilian courts (TJGO expert panel) and international arbitration bodies.
What does ISO 18738 ride quality compliance mean for my property?
ISO 18738 defines measurable acceptance limits for elevator ride comfort: horizontal vibration A95 ≤ 15 milli-g, vertical vibration, noise inside the cab ≤ 55 dB(A), jerk, and leveling accuracy. Compliance protects property owners from tenant complaints, legal liability, and ADA/accessibility disputes. Our FFT report shows each parameter against the ISO 18738 threshold, providing objective evidence for maintenance contract renegotiations and modernization decisions.
Can your report support due diligence and maintenance contract disputes?
Yes. Our report is structured for Elevator Due Diligence, Vertical Transportation Audit, Latent defects inspection, and Maintenance contract compliance review. It is frequently used by lawyers, insurers, and property managers to challenge excessive repair claims, validate technical causation, and document non-conformities with legal-grade engineering evidence.
Can your inspection report be used as legal evidence in the US or internationally?
Yes. Our reports are bilingual (Portuguese + English), include calibrated measurement data with RBC traceability, FFT spectral charts, synchronized 360° inspection video, and ART-CREA certification. Engineer Georgio Batista de Lima is listed in the CREA/TJGO expert witness panel. This documentation package meets the standard of evidence for Brazilian courts, and serves as prima facie technical evidence in international commercial arbitration (ICC, UNCITRAL) involving elevator OEMs such as Otis, Schindler, KONE, ThyssenKrupp, and Atlas Schindler.
Why shouldn't I use the elevator's own maintenance company for inspection?
This is a fundamental conflict of interest. Maintenance companies profit from repair and parts sales — meaning their inspections are inherently biased toward findings that justify their service contracts. Proton Engineering earns revenue exclusively from independent diagnostics. We manufacture nothing, sell no parts, and hold no maintenance contracts. Additionally, traditional maintenance technicians rely on subjective sensory assessment, while we deploy laboratory-grade instruments measuring vibration (0.1 µm precision) and calibrated acoustic sensors — detecting failures months before human perception.

We inspect all elevator types: traction (geared/gearless, MRL), hydraulic, freight/cargo, panoramic/observation, platform accessibility lifts, escalators and moving walks. All major global brands: Otis, ThyssenKrupp TKE, Schindler, KONE, Hyundai, Mitsubishi, Fujitec, Atlas Schindler, Novael, Villarta, and all other manufacturers present in the Brazilian market. We also handle elevators from discontinued brands and post-modernization mixed-technology units.
