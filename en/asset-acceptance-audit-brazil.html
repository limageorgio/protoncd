Why It Matters

Acceptance Is Not
A Signature. It Is Evidence.

Once the contractor demobilizes, every undocumented defect becomes a debate. The baseline audit creates the factual reference that warranty, maintenance and insurance conversations require.

What a baseline protects

  • Warranty start — a documented condition at the agreed acceptance date, not a date remembered months later.
  • Defects liability — a clear boundary between pre-existing conditions and damage caused after handover.
  • Maintenance transition — asset register, open items and technical observations ready for the maintenance provider.
  • Insurance and claims — contemporaneous evidence of condition, tests and limitations.
  • Future disputes — findings linked to requirements, measurements and photographs instead of opinions.
  • Capital planning — a realistic starting point for lifecycle and replacement budgets.

The cost of a missing baseline

Without a zero-point record, the owner often pays twice: once for the defect and again for the inability to prove when it appeared. The audit is a small fraction of that exposure.

Audit Scope

Four Dimensions
of Acceptance

The audit does not stop at visual inspection. Acceptance requires documents, condition, function and performance to align with the same requirement set.

Documentation & Conformity

  • Approved design and as-built drawings
  • Equipment certificates, manuals and data sheets
  • ART, CREA registrations and technical responsibility records
  • Test reports, calibration certificates and material certificates
  • Operation and maintenance manuals in Portuguese

Installed Condition

  • Asset identification and location register
  • Mechanical completion, alignment, fixing and accessibility
  • Electrical connections, labeling and protection devices
  • Finishes, corrosion, leakage and housekeeping
  • Access for operation, inspection and maintenance

Functional & Safety Tests

  • Safety devices and interlocks
  • Emergency systems and fail-safe behavior
  • Control sequences, alarms and signaling
  • Load, pressure, flow or performance tests as applicable
  • Witnessed tests and hold points

Performance & Comfort

  • Instrumented vibration, noise or temperature measurements
  • Ride quality and operational comfort where applicable
  • Energy and capacity indicators against design intent
  • Trend comparison between assets of the same lot
  • Objective criteria instead of subjective impression

Adaptable to any asset class

Elevators, HVAC, fire protection, pumping systems, electrical substations, gas networks, industrial machinery, building automation or complete facilities. The technical matrix changes; the acceptance logic — requirement, evidence, verification, decision — does not.

Evidence Hierarchy

From Documents
to Witnessed Tests

Evidence has weight. A document proves intent; a witnessed test proves behavior. The audit combines levels according to risk and access.

Level 1–2: documents and visual inspection

Fast, broad and essential for completeness. They establish conformity of identity, configuration, access and finish — but they cannot prove dynamic behavior.

Level 3: instrumented measurement

Quantifies vibration, noise, pressure, flow, temperature, insulation resistance or other variables with calibrated equipment and documented conditions.

Level 4: functional and safety testing

Demonstrates that the asset performs its intended function and responds correctly to failure, emergency and interlock scenarios.

Level 5: witnessed acceptance

The strongest evidence: the test is executed under agreed conditions and observed by an independent technical representative, with signed records.

Method

Five Steps
to a Defensible Acceptance

A disciplined sequence that starts with requirements, not with a clipboard.

01 · Kick-off & document review

Contract requirements, approved design, applicable standards, test plans, as-built status and open punch items. Output: acceptance criteria matrix and document gap list.

02 · Sampling plan

Risk-based selection of assets, systems, locations and tests. Critical items are verified fully; repetitive items are sampled with a defined confidence logic. Output: approved sampling plan.

03 · Field campaign

Documented inspection, measurements, witnessed tests, photographic evidence and daily debriefs. Access limitations and deviations from the plan are recorded. Output: field evidence package.

04 · Findings & classification

Each deviation is linked to a requirement and classified by severity and acceptance impact. Output: findings register with evidence, responsibility and recommended action.

05 · Report, ART & baseline dossier

Final validation report, registered ART, executive summary in English and Portuguese, and the zero-point dossier. Output: an acceptance decision the owner can defend.

Acceptance Decisions

Four Possible
Outcomes

The report does not end with "approved" or "rejected". It states the decision, the conditions, the evidence and the next step.

Decision Criteria Commercial / operational effect Next step
Accepted All critical and major findings closed; documentation complete; performance criteria met within the agreed tolerances. Asset released for operation; warranty and defects-liability periods start as contractually defined. Baseline dossier issued; open observations tracked as routine maintenance items.
Accepted with reservations No open critical findings; limited major items with an owner-approved corrective plan, deadline and adequate guarantee or retention. Conditional release; the owner retains leverage until verification of the agreed items. Follow-up verification date; closure evidence added to the dossier.
Not accepted Open critical finding, unresolved major item affecting safety or performance, or essential documentation missing. Acceptance withheld for the affected system; warranty start may be postponed as contractually applicable. Corrective-action plan, re-inspection and revised acceptance date.
Inconclusive Access, documentation or test conditions insufficient to reach an evidence-based conclusion. Decision suspended; no inference of conformity or non-conformity. Scope adjustment, additional access or complementary testing.

Decision labels are illustrative of the methodology. Contractual effects are governed by the applicable contract and legal advice.

Execution Models

One Campaign
or Phased Lots

The same method can be executed as a single concentrated campaign or split into phased lots. The choice depends on portfolio size, access, operational windows and cash-flow preferences.

Phased campaign

Assets are grouped into lots and accepted progressively.

  • Earlier acceptance for completed areas
  • Spread of cost and site mobilization
  • Faster feedback to the contractor per lot
  • Requires consistent criteria across lots
  • Baseline dates may differ by lot

Single campaign

All assets are inspected in one concentrated mobilization.

  • One consistent baseline date for the portfolio
  • Single report and executive summary
  • Efficient use of specialist team and instruments
  • Higher peak resource demand
  • Depends on simultaneous access and completion

Plan the hold points before the last installation phase

Critical tests are cheaper to witness than to reconstruct. When the audit scope is agreed early, Proton can define hold points, attend key tests and avoid a second mobilization to prove what was already covered.

Maintenance Transition

The Baseline Is Also
A Tender Document

When the owner can describe the asset objectively, maintenance bidders price the real scope — not the uncertainty.

  • Common factual basis — every bidder evaluates the same asset register and documented condition.
  • Lower uncertainty premium — fewer contingencies for unknown pre-existing defects.
  • Clear responsibility boundary — defects existing at handover are separated from damage during the maintenance period.
  • Scope definition — preventive and corrective activities can be priced against actual equipment and condition.
  • Spare-parts planning — identification and criticality support inventory decisions.
  • Performance monitoring — baseline indicators become the reference for maintenance KPIs.

Scope Limits

What the Audit
Does Not Promise

Trust grows when limits are explicit. These statements are part of the report, not a footnote hidden at the end.

Hidden defects

The audit is non-destructive unless additional testing is contracted. Defects requiring disassembly or destructive examination may not be detectable.

Sampling

Repetitive items are verified by sampling. A sample result is not a guarantee that every identical unit has the same condition.

Point in time

The report reflects the conditions and access available on the inspection dates. Later events, use or modifications are outside its scope.

Not a manufacturer warranty

The audit verifies conformity at acceptance. It does not extend, replace or modify the manufacturer's warranty.

Not a design review

Design adequacy is verified only against the agreed acceptance criteria. A full design review requires a separate scope.

Not legal advice

The report provides technical evidence. Contractual interpretation, notices and claims remain with the owner and its legal counsel.
